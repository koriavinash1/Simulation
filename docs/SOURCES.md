# External reference points for blue finance

These references motivate the **structure** of the finance module; they do not make the simulator defaults universally valid.

1. World Bank, *Seychelles: Introducing the World's First Sovereign Blue Bond* (2025 case study). The sovereign blue bond financed marine protection and sustainable fisheries through a Blue Grants Fund and Blue Investment Fund. The World Bank case study reports that guarantees/concessional support reduced sovereign borrowing costs and that proceeds were channelled as grants and loans.
   - https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/cbaf1cefc5164a7f340716ef0af6fd7e-0340012025/original/Case-Study-Blue-Bond-Seychelles.pdf

2. Development Bank of Seychelles, Blue Investment Fund. Current published terms include US$10,000-US$3 million loans, 4% interest, up to 15 years, up to 90% financing, focused on sustainable fisheries value chains including managed pelagic fisheries such as tuna and billfish.
   - https://dbs.sc/service/bif-scheme/

3. Development Bank of Seychelles, Blue Finance overview. Describes the Blue Grants Fund and Blue Investment Fund and the objective of financing the transition to sustainable fisheries, governance, value addition and market options.
   - https://dbs.sc/blue-finance/

4. World Bank, *Accelerating Blue Finance: Instruments, Case Studies, and Pathways to Scale*. Describes blue bonds, debt-for-nature swaps, guarantees and insurance as instruments used to mobilize capital for ocean priorities.
   - https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/environment/publication/accelerating-blue-finance-instruments-case-studies-and-pathways-to-scale

## Modeling caution

World Bank reporting also notes that small-scale uptake can be constrained by application complexity and collateral requirements. Therefore the simulator includes explicit eligibility thresholds and finite program reach rather than assuming all fishers can access concessional capital.
